# Transport acceptance

Use dummy people. The owner cannot test phones today (23 October 2026).
The earlier NFC pass belongs to its recorded binaries; it does not pass the
new BLE candidate. Automated video-codec checks do not pass a real call.

## Bluetooth on the two phones

1. Install the candidate whose hashes are recorded in `evidence/transport-artifacts.json`.
   Keep the existing encrypted vaults. Open Connect and complete normal device unlock.
2. Allow Bluetooth and keep the apps foreground. Leave **Tap using NFC** untouched.
3. Both must reach the same four words without typing or manual retry. Compare
   them, name each other and save separately. Record the time and exact stage if
   one phone waits. Neither proximity nor a one-sided result is a pass.
4. Repeat with the other phone starting first. Interrupt Bluetooth once, restore
   it, and check recovery. A prolonged interruption may hit the one-minute pause;
   **Try nearby again** must continue the same saved exchange.
5. Lock/background either app during a new exchange. Radios must stop. Unlock
   and resume, then finish separately. Reopen both saved people and check normal,
   wrong and agreed duress words in both directions.

Retain only model/OS, candidate hashes, operation stages, timing and human
outcome. Never record live words, secrets, PINs or private people in logs/photos.
Debug logs distinguish discovery, service readiness, full-frame reads and timeouts;
they cannot establish matching words or that both humans saved.

## A real trusted video call

Use two camera devices and a second screen each camera can see, with two dummy
people and an already trusted call. Keep vault/call/recovery screens out of the
call. A phone cannot scan its own call screen; use the public link/encrypted-file
fallback in that case.

1. Make a new pair using **Connect on a call** and the moving QR invitation.
   The receiver returns its encrypted reply QR. Compare all four fixed words
   through the trusted call and save on each device.
2. Repeat with roles reversed. Record provider, devices, browser/native versions,
   camera used, approximate connection quality and where reading stalls.
3. Copy a dummy saved person from phone to desktop and back: public receiving
   request towards the source, encrypted copy towards the receiver. Compare the
   import code before importing; check originals, duplicates and unlock survive.
4. Try ordinary call quality and a poor connection. Cover the QR briefly or move
   the camera away; progress must resume when parts repeat. Try **Trouble scanning? →
   Make QR bigger and slower**. Never infer success from partial progress.
5. Leave the reader or lock the device; capture must stop. After the three-minute
   reading pause, reopen the same saved exchange. Report failure if a new pair
   is needed, a transfer is mixed, the wrong person is imported, or the user is lost.

Record each outcome separately. Real camera focus/glare, provider screen-sharing,
video compression, app switching, system unlock and novice/panic usability remain
physical acceptance gates even when automated codec and native builds pass.
